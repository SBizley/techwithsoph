Prompt basics

When I asked what people needed the most help with, this beat everything else, then it turned up again anyway, buried in the answers nobody prompted for it. So here's the whole thing properly, not a rushed paragraph at the bottom of another page.

Why your first go usually falls flat

Type "write me an email" and you'll get an email. A completely generic one, the kind anyone could have written, because that's all it had to go on. It isn't reading your mind, it's reading exactly what you typed, word for word. Vague in, vague out, every single time.

The four things a good prompt needs

Not a course, just these four. Use whichever ones are relevant, in whatever order makes sense for what you're asking.

Context

Tell it what it's working with

Who you are, what you're trying to do, what you've already tried. "I'm a nurse writing a handover note" gets you somewhere completely different to nothing at all on the subject.

Outcome

Say what good looks like

Not "help me plan a party", more "a party for twelve, in my flat, on a Saturday, that doesn't need me cooking on the day." The more specific the outcome, the less it has to guess.

Format

Tell it what shape you want back

A list, a table, three sentences, something you could read aloud. Say nothing and you'll usually get three paragraphs whether you wanted them or not.

Constraints

Give it the boundaries

A budget, a deadline, a tone, a length. "Keep it under 100 words" or "nothing over £50" does more work than another sentence of explanation.

Same task, two different prompts

Same person, same email, wildly different result depending on what you type.

Asking for help with a work email

The vague version:

Write me an email to my manager about needing more time.

The specific version:

I need an email to my manager, [name], asking for an extra week on the [project] deadline. I'm behind because [real reason]. I want to sound accountable, not like I'm making excuses. Keep it under 150 words and suggest a new date.

The first gets you something generic you'll end up rewriting anyway. The second gets you something close to what you'd send. Try both on the same thing and see the gap for yourself.

My honest take: you don't need to learn "prompt engineering", that's a job title, not a life skill. You just need to stop assuming it can read your mind, and tell it what you're picturing. More on why vague prompts trip people up in Watchouts.

Prompt templates to copy and paste

A few that work for almost anything, not just the examples above. Copy one, fill in the brackets, go.

Tidy something up

Turn something rough into something usable

Here's a rough version of [what you're working on]: [paste it in]. Tidy it up, keep my voice, and tell me if anything's confusing or missing.
Understand something

Get a straight explanation

Explain [topic or thing you don't understand] like I'm smart but new to this. No jargon, and if you have to use a technical word, explain it in the same sentence.
Before you decide

Check your own thinking

I'm planning to [decision or plan]. Here's my reasoning: [your reasoning]. Poke holes in it. Tell me what I'm missing or what could go wrong.
Get started

Get a first draft, not a lecture

Write me a first draft of [thing you need, e.g. a speech, a bio, a job description]. Here's what it needs to cover: [key points]. Keep it in my voice, not a generic version.

Set your AI up once, instead of repeating yourself

Most AI tools let you save instructions that apply to every chat, so you're not explaining who you are every time. In ChatGPT it's under Settings, then Custom instructions. In Claude it's in your Profile settings. Paste something like this in once:

I'm [your role or context, e.g. a nurse, a small business owner, a parent going back to work after a break]. When you answer me: keep it practical, skip jargon or explain it plainly if you have to use it, keep answers as short as they can be while still being useful, and tell me clearly if something needs a real professional instead of a guess.

Update it whenever it stops fitting. It's not permanent, just a starting point that saves you typing the same thing every time.
