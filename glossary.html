The words people throw around, actually explained

Nobody explains these properly, they just assume you already know them. You don't need to memorise this, just come back whenever someone uses one of these words at you. Start with beginner, only go further if you're curious.

Beginner

The words you'll hit in your first week of actually using an AI tool.

Prompt
Just the thing you type or say to an AI tool. That's it. "Write me an email" is a prompt. There's no special skill required to have one, though a more specific prompt gets you a more useful answer.
Hallucination
When an AI states something confidently that's just wrong. It won't say "I'm not sure", it'll say something false as if it's fact. This is the single biggest reason to double-check anything that actually matters (numbers, dates, legal or medical specifics) before you rely on it.
Token
Roughly, a small chunk of text, think part of a word. You'll mostly see this word when a tool talks about limits or pricing. You don't need to understand the mechanics, just know it's the unit these tools measure text in.
Chat tool
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini in their basic form. You ask a question, you get an answer back. Nothing happens on its own, it waits for you.
App builder
Tools like Lovable. You describe what you want built (an app, a website, a tracker) in plain English, and it actually builds a working version, no coding needed from you.
Agent (or "Cowork-style" tool)
The step up from chat. Instead of one question and one answer, it can carry out several steps on your behalf, over time, sometimes without you sitting there watching. Example I use myself: a scheduled task that posts a summary to my team on a set day, pulling from documents I've already given it, running even when I'm not at my desk.
Context
The background information an AI tool has about you or your situation. The more relevant context you give it upfront (what you do, what you're trying to achieve, even your CV or a project brief), the less generic and more useful its answers get.
Model
The actual engine doing the thinking behind a tool. You'll sometimes see version numbers or names thrown around (like different model versions inside ChatGPT). Mostly you can ignore this, the tool itself matters far more day to day than which exact model sits behind it.
Projects
A dedicated space inside a tool like Claude where you keep one thing's chats, files and instructions together. Instead of starting cold every time you open a new conversation, it already knows what this project is about.
Artifacts
A separate, standalone thing Claude builds for you inside a chat, a document, a webpage, a spreadsheet, that sits outside the back-and-forth. You can open it, edit it, come back to it later, rather than scrolling up trying to find what it said three messages ago.
Skills
An add-on instruction pack you give an AI tool to make it noticeably better at one specific job. I've used one to check a website for accessibility problems, and every person I've shown it to had no idea this was even possible.
Scheduled task (automation)
The thing that makes "agent" above actually useful day to day: telling a tool to run something on its own, on a schedule, a weekly summary, a reminder, a task done while you're not even at your desk.
Custom instructions
The upfront rules you give a tool about how to talk to you and what to remember, your job, your situation, your preferences, so every answer after that is shaped around you instead of generic.
GitHub
Not actually an AI term, but it comes up the second you start building anything with AI. It's where code and project files get stored and tracked over time, basically a saved history of every change. Most AI coding tools save their work there.
The one thing worth actually remembering: start from the outcome you want, then work backward to what you type. Don't start by trying to write the "perfect" prompt, start by knowing what a good result would actually look like.

Intermediate

Once the beginner words feel normal, this is the next layer, still practical, not engineering talk.

Connectors
Links an AI tool to an app you already use, Gmail, Notion, whatever, so it can read from or act inside that app directly instead of you copying and pasting between the two.
Evals
Short for evaluations. A test used to check how reliable an AI actually is, run against a set of questions with known right answers and scored on how well it does. Think report card, not magic.
Context window
How much a tool can hold onto at once in one conversation, everything you've said plus everything it's said back. Go past it and the earliest parts start dropping off, which is why a long chat can suddenly seem to forget something you told it ages ago.
Multimodal
A tool that works with more than text, photos, voice, documents, sometimes video. If you've ever spoken into a tool or handed it a screenshot instead of typing, that's multimodal doing its job.
LLM (Large Language Model)
The specific type of AI behind tools like ChatGPT and Claude, trained on huge amounts of text to predict and generate language. When people say "the AI", this is usually what they actually mean.
API
A way for two pieces of software to talk to each other automatically, no person clicking anything in between. It's the plumbing that lets an AI tool plug into another app behind the scenes.
Fine-tuning
Taking a model that already works and training it further on a narrower set of examples so it gets noticeably better at one particular job.
