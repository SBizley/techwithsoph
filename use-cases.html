Pick one, try it now

Five real things to try today

Not ten tips, not a course. One real task, done properly, teaches you more than a week of reading about AI. Pick whichever of these matches something you're actually dealing with, copy the prompt, swap in your own details, and see what comes back.

Travel & events

Plan a trip when you don't know where to start

This is the one I used on my own mum, she wanted to plan a trip and had no idea where to begin. Give it a destination, some dates, and a budget, and it'll build you an actual day-by-day plan you can adjust.

I'm planning a trip to [destination] for [number] days in [month]. My budget is roughly [amount]. I'm travelling with [who]. Build me a day-by-day itinerary with a mix of the big must-see things and some quieter local spots, and flag anything that needs booking in advance.
I've got a rough plan for [destination] already: [paste what you have, even if it's messy]. Look at it and tell me what's missing, what's overambitious for the time I've got, and what order actually makes sense given the locations.
Money

Get on top of your finances using your own numbers

Not generic budgeting advice, your actual numbers. Export a bank statement or just list out what you spend, and ask it to build you something you can actually use and come back to.

Here's what I spend in a typical month: [list your income and outgoings, or paste a bank statement export]. Build me a simple monthly budget, tell me where the money's actually going, and suggest where I could realistically cut back without me having to give up everything I enjoy.
I want to save [amount] for [goal] by [date]. Based on what I've told you about my spending, work out what I'd need to save each month to hit that, and tell me honestly whether it's realistic.

Worth knowing: don't paste in full account numbers or sort codes, you don't need them for this and it's just not necessary information to hand over. See risks & opportunities for more on this.

Work & life admin

Draft the email you're avoiding

Everyone has one. A message that needs the right tone and you keep putting off because you don't know how to phrase it.

I need to write an email to [who] about [what's happening]. I want to come across as [e.g. firm but fair / apologetic but clear / friendly but professional]. Here's roughly what I want to say: [your rough notes, doesn't need to be polished]. Write me a draft, and keep it short, nobody wants to read three paragraphs.
Start here if nothing else

Automate one thing you do by hand every week

This is my one piece of advice, repeated because it matters: don't try to use AI for everything at once. Find one repetitive thing you do manually and hand it over.

Every week I have to [describe the repetitive task, e.g. "write a summary of what happened for my team" or "go through receipts and work out what to claim back"]. Here's what that usually involves: [describe your current process]. Suggest a simpler way to do this, and if it's something you can help me build (like a template, a checklist, or a small tracker), build it with me now.
Big decisions

Make sense of a big life-admin decision

A move, a mortgage, a big purchase, the kind of decision where you don't even know what questions to ask yet.

I'm trying to decide whether to [describe the decision, e.g. "remortgage now or wait", "buy or lease a car", "move to a new area"]. Here's what I know so far: [your situation and any numbers you have]. Don't just give me an answer, tell me what questions I should actually be asking, and what I'd need to find out before I could decide properly.

Use this to get organised and ask better questions, not as a replacement for real financial or legal advice on anything big. More on that in risks & opportunities.
