Before you get started

Watchouts

This isn't a disclaimer I'm burying at the bottom of the page. When I asked people what worries them about AI, the same handful of things came up constantly, so they get proper space here, alongside what I think genuinely makes it worth using anyway.

What to watch for

Prompting
You have to actually tell it what you want. A vague prompt gets you a vague, generic answer. If you want a specific tone, a specific format, or a specific outcome, say so upfront. It's not reading your mind, it's reading what you actually typed.
Accuracy
It gets things wrong, confidently. An AI tool won't always say "I'm not sure", it'll sometimes state something false as if it's fact. Double-check anything that actually matters, numbers, dates, anything legal, medical, or financial, before you rely on it.
Privacy
What you type isn't private in the way a notebook is. Assume anything you paste into a free tool could, in some form, be used to improve the product. Don't put in full account numbers, passwords, or anything you wouldn't want to see outside your own head. This came up in almost every honest answer I got when I asked people what worries them.
Cost
Free tiers run out faster than you'd think. Ask for one big, complicated thing, several documents at once, a long back-and-forth, a big rewrite, and you can hit your limit before you're actually done. And it's not a permanent promise either: today's generous free access is there to get you using the tool, not guaranteed to stay this generous.
Sycophancy
It's built to be agreeable. One thing that stuck with me from someone I surveyed: "the positivity to every idea, it's built to please the user." That's a fair criticism. Push back on it, ask it to find the holes in your own idea, don't just take encouragement at face value.
Environment
There's a real cost behind it. Running these tools uses real computing power, and that has a genuine environmental footprint, along with the more obvious financial one once you're past a free tier. Worth knowing, not a reason for guilt over asking it to help plan your Tuesday.

Why I still think it's worth it

Not "AI will change your life." More specifically: the one repetitive thing you hand over properly, planning a trip, sorting a budget, drafting the emails you avoid, gives back real hours you'd have spent doing it manually.

You don't need a team, a budget, or a technical background to get real help with a project, a business idea, or a big decision.

That kind of access didn't really exist five years ago. It's a genuine equaliser.

And it transfers faster than you'd think. Getting properly comfortable with one tool, one real task, tends to make the next one far less intimidating. The hard part is the first one, not the tenth.

My honest take: the risk isn't AI itself, it's using it uncritically. Treat it like a very capable colleague who's sometimes wrong and always agreeable, useful, but not something you switch your brain off for.
